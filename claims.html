Health Insurance Claims: What Happens Between Hospital Billing and Payment?

A claim can be cashless, approved and still leave the policyholder with an amount to pay. Understanding the claim process helps explain why.

What Is a Health Insurance Claim?

A health insurance claim is the process through which the policyholder or hospital asks the insurer to pay eligible medical expenses under the terms of the policy.

The important point is that claim approval does not automatically mean that every amount on the hospital bill is payable by the insurer.

The final payment can depend on the policy's coverage, exclusions, limits, deductibles, co-payments, waiting periods and the nature of the expenses in the bill.

Two Common Types of Claims

The process can differ depending on whether the hospital settles the eligible amount directly with the insurer or the policyholder pays first.

Cashless Claim

The hospital coordinates with the insurer or claims administrator for eligible treatment expenses. The policyholder may still need to pay amounts that are not covered or are subject to policy conditions.

Reimbursement Claim

The policyholder pays the hospital bill and later submits the required documents for reimbursement of eligible expenses according to the policy terms.

Claim Settlement

The amount finally paid depends on the admissible expenses and applicable policy terms, not simply on the total hospital bill.

How a Claim Typically Moves

The exact process varies by insurer and policy, but these stages help explain the basic journey.

1

Hospital Admission or Treatment

The patient receives treatment and the hospital records services, medicines, room charges, procedures and other expenses.

2

Claim Intimation

The insurer or claims administrator is informed according to the applicable policy process and timelines.

3

Document and Eligibility Review

The claim is reviewed against the policy terms, available documents and treatment details.

4

Approval or Assessment

The insurer may approve an amount, request more information, or assess deductions and non-payable expenses.

5

Final Settlement

The insurer pays the eligible amount according to the policy. Any non-covered or otherwise payable amount may remain with the policyholder.

Why Can an Approved Claim Still Leave a Payment?

This is one of the most common areas of confusion for policyholders.

Non-Payable Items

Some items or expenses in a hospital bill may not be covered under the applicable policy terms.

Room-Rent Limits

Where a policy has a room-rent or related limit, the settlement may be affected according to the policy wording.

Co-Payment

If a co-payment condition applies, the policyholder may have to bear a specified share of eligible expenses.

Deductible

A deductible can require the policyholder to bear an initial amount before the insurer becomes liable under the policy.

Sub-Limits

A policy may place specific limits on certain treatments or expenses, subject to the exact policy terms.

Exclusions & Waiting Periods

Some conditions or treatments may be excluded or may not be payable during an applicable waiting period.

Before You Accept a Claim Settlement

Examine the settlement information and hospital bill together rather than looking only at the approval amount.

  • Compare the final hospital bill with the amount approved or settled.
  • Identify deductions and ask what policy condition applies to each material deduction.
  • Check whether non-payable items have been listed separately.
  • Check applicable room-rent, co-payment, deductible and sub-limit conditions.
  • Keep copies of bills, discharge summary, settlement statement and important correspondence.
  • If something is unclear, ask the insurer or claims administrator for a written explanation.

Real-World Claim Questions

These are the kinds of questions this Lab will examine through practical examples.

“Cashless Was Approved. Why Did I Still Pay?”

Approval and full payment of the hospital bill are not necessarily the same thing.

“Why Is the Insurer's Amount Lower Than the Bill?”

The difference may reflect policy conditions and expenses that are not payable under the applicable terms.

“Where Did the Deduction Come From?”

A settlement statement can be easier to understand when each major deduction is connected to the relevant policy condition.

This page provides general educational information. Health insurance coverage and claim settlement depend on the specific policy wording, applicable terms and individual claim circumstances.
