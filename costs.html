Out-of-Pocket Costs

Health insurance can reduce the financial burden of hospital treatment, but an approved claim does not always mean that the entire hospital bill will be paid by the insurer.

Why Can You Still Have to Pay?

The amount paid by the insurer can differ from the total hospital bill because of policy conditions, limits, exclusions, deductions and items that are not payable under the policy.

Understanding these factors before treatment can make the final claim settlement easier to understand.

Common Sources of Out-of-Pocket Costs

Policy Limits

Room-rent limits, sub-limits and other internal limits can restrict the amount payable for particular expenses.

Non-Payable Items

Some hospital bill items may not be payable under the policy even when the main treatment is covered.

Co-payment

Where a co-payment condition applies, the policyholder may have to bear a specified share of eligible expenses.

Deductibles

A deductible can require the policyholder to bear an initial amount before the insurer becomes liable according to the policy terms.

Exclusions

Expenses falling under policy exclusions are generally not payable, subject to the exact wording and applicable conditions.

Package & Billing Differences

Hospital billing structures and insurer-approved amounts can differ, creating a gap between the billed amount and the settled amount.

A Simple Way to Think About the Final Bill

Total Hospital Bill

− Amount not payable under policy conditions

− Deductions, limits or co-payment where applicable

= Amount paid by insurer

Any remaining amount may become the patient's responsibility, depending on the policy and claim circumstances.

What to Check Before Hospitalisation

  • Room-rent and related limits
  • Sub-limits for specific treatments or expenses
  • Co-payment and deductible conditions
  • Important exclusions and waiting periods
  • Whether common hospital-bill items are payable
  • How cashless approval differs from final claim settlement
