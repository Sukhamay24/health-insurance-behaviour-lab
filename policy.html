Policy Design & Limits

What Your Health Insurance Policy Limits Can Change

A health insurance policy is not defined only by its sum insured. Room-rent limits, sub-limits, co-payments, deductibles, waiting periods and exclusions can affect what is actually payable during a claim.

Why Policy Structure Matters

The same hospital bill can produce different claim outcomes under different policy conditions. Understanding the policy structure before treatment can make the claim process easier to understand.

Room-Rent Limits

A policy may place a limit on the eligible room category. Depending on the policy wording and claim calculation, choosing a room above the permitted limit can affect more than just the room charge.

Sub-Limits

Some policies may contain separate limits for particular treatments, procedures or expenses. These limits can restrict the amount payable even when the overall sum insured is higher.

Co-Payment

A co-payment clause may require the policyholder to bear a specified percentage of eligible claim expenses. The exact effect depends on the policy terms.

Deductibles

A deductible is an amount that may need to be borne before the insurer becomes liable according to the policy terms. It can change the amount payable by the insurer.

Waiting Periods

Certain conditions or treatments may have waiting periods. Whether a claim is payable can therefore depend on when the policy started and the applicable policy wording.

Exclusions

Some expenses, conditions or treatments may be excluded from coverage. A claim being submitted or a treatment being medically necessary does not by itself mean every expense is payable.

One Important Question Before Treatment

Do not look only at the sum insured.

Before planned hospitalisation, examine the relevant policy conditions: room eligibility, sub-limits, co-payment, deductible, waiting periods, exclusions and other applicable limits.

From Policy Wording to Claim Outcome

The practical claim outcome is influenced by the relationship between the hospital bill and the policy conditions. This is why a policy with a large sum insured can still leave some expenses payable by the policyholder.
